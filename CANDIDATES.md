# Worst app pitch candidates (draft pass)

One sentence each, at most 240 characters: pitch the worst,
most useless app imaginable; judged on failing brilliantly.

1. Napkin: "Introducing Napkin: a subscription app that
   sends you one photo of a napkin daily; premium unlocks
   the same napkin in landscape." -- a complete business
   model of nothing; the landscape tier is inspired.
2. Queue: "Meet Queue: wait in a virtual line to be told
   the thing you queued for is available instantly
   elsewhere; the Pro tier waits longer." -- monetised
   waiting; arguably more brilliant a failure than Napkin.
3. Almost: "Almost is a navigation app that gets you within
   one street of your destination and wishes you luck." --
   near-miss as a service.
4. Echo Mail: "Echo Mail forwards every email you send back
   to you, so you can experience your own tone." -- a mirror
   with a subscription.
5. Snooze Button: "An alarm app whose only feature is a
   snooze button that charges your card per snooze, payable
   to your gym." -- punitive, almost useful, disqualified
   by usefulness.

## Verdict

Winner: candidate 2. The judge rewards the pitch that fails
most brilliantly, and Queue fails with a business model:
the product is waiting, the revelation is that the waiting
was unnecessary, and the premium tier -- the detail that
wins it -- sells you a longer wait. Napkin (candidate 1) is
purer nothing; Queue is nothing, monetised at two tiers.
